🐉 Maria's Warm-Up

Spin, choose and chat!
🌀
Spin to start! 👉
Choose an idea or give your own. Say a sentence, then ask me!
Teacher notes

Use 2 or 3 spins for a 5-minute warm-up. Start by asking Maria for today's date. Every card is a real chat: she picks a button or gives her own answer, says a whole sentence, then you ask one question from «What else?». Wait for her to build the sentence before helping. Two cards carry the test's oral questions in everyday form, so you can switch into the examiner on them: My family (introduce your family) and Languages (why join the section; «one more language» sits on a starter). My family has a new question, because she answered «Who do you look like?» on 18 November (her father); the hair starter keeps the size word before the gap (short / long ... hair), for the adjective order from 4 November. Languages has a new question too (she chose Italian on 18 November); her reason, «like French», came after my prompt, so the starters leave the reason open. Treehouse comes from the treehouse series she is reading (18 November) and now carries «There are lots of...» for the test writing. Best day replaces Free time: «On Wednesdays I...» is there for her «all Wednesdays», and if she says her favourite thing is existing, that is her joke, so enjoy it and ask what else. Helping comes from 18 November («I help my cat, my dog and my mom», «I don't know how to...») and stays in the first person. Jobs and Scary things have not been spun yet. Jobs stays in the first person too (I'd like to be..., I'd like to work in...): she already said dentist on 11 November, so open with «Would you like to work with animals or with people?». Scary things: the starters show «I'm scared of...» for how you feel and «... is scary» for the thing. Food asks what kind of food she likes best: let her explain French words in English, and its «It's...» starter is a chance to hear «it» for a thing. Wild places comes from the magazine (arctic animals on 4 November, Peru and llamas on 6 November). No card asks what somebody does (a pilot, her dog, her cat, her mum or dad): keep «What does ... do?» for the lesson. No card is about her dog, about Katie or about the detective costume: those words come back from pictures in the lesson. Recast her French-way forms in the chat when they come up, then let her say the sentence again herself. A personal answer does not have to match a button. Invite her to ask you a question too.
